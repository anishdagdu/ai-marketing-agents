## 1. What are Carry1st's business lines and how does each make money?

### Takeaway
As of Nov 2026 Carry1st presents itself as a "commerce platform for Africa and the Middle East" with four partner-facing products: Publishing, Pay1st (payments, merchant of record), the Carry1st Shop (D2C storefront for game currency, gift cards and vouchers), and a newer B2B distribution API called Flow1st. The site's weight is on the Shop and payments; first-party games have almost disappeared from the navigation.

### Cited Findings

**Positioning**
- Aug 25, 2026 press boilerplate: Carry1st is "a commerce platform for Africa and the Middle East, operating across games, publishing, and fintech"; products named are Pay1st and the Carry1st Shop; it "has scaled Call of Duty: Mobile, VALORANT, and Mergedom"; founded 2018 — [Carry1st press, Aug 25 2026](https://www.carry1st.com/press/africas-games-market-set-to-outpace-global-growth-in-2026)

### Inferences
- Every corporate page ends in a Shop app download or "Shop now" CTA, and the one senior growth hire is scoped to the Shop. The Shop is the consumer growth priority; Pay1st and Flow1st are the B2B monetisation layers on the same catalogue and payment rails.
- Flow1st extends the model from selling to gamers to wholesaling to telcos and fintechs, and explicitly names LATAM, which is outside Carry1st's historic footprint.
- The blog is run as an SEO acquisition channel for the Shop (game-plus-currency keywords such as CP, UC, diamonds, redeem codes), not as corporate comms.
- The corporate site is under-maintained (Lorem ipsum, dead Games link, 404 on /games, stale events, mismatched copyright years, conflicting Pay1st counts). That is a conversion and credibility leak for B2B leads.

- Nigeria and South Africa are the test markets for new consumer channels (WhatsApp Shop, campus communities, Independence Day promos); Egypt is the largest African market by revenue and the bridge into MENA.
