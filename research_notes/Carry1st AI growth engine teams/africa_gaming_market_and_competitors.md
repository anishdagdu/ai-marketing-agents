# African Mobile Gaming, Digital-Content Payments Market and Carry1st Competitive Landscape (2025-2026)

Research date: 2026-11-14. Every figure below carries the year it describes and the edition it comes from. Most market figures were taken from secondary coverage of the Carry1st/Newzoo, Maliyo/KPMG, GeoPoll and SpielFabrique/Xsolla reports, not from the primary PDFs; treat them as "as reported by" figures. Where sources conflict, both values are shown.

## 1. How large is the African games market (revenue, gamers, growth) by country?

### Takeaway
Africa is a roughly $1.8-2.0B games market with about 370-406M gamers, growing about 10-12% a year and 80-90% mobile; three countries (Egypt, South Africa, Nigeria) make up roughly half of revenue. Estimates differ widely between publishers ($1.0B to $2.29B for similar years), and the Carry1st/Newzoo series itself restated 2025 below its earlier 2024 figure, so any single number needs its edition attached.

### Cited Findings

Continent totals (Carry1st/Newzoo series)
- 2024 (report published Feb 2025): $1.8B revenue, up 12.4% YoY versus 2.1% global growth; 349M gamers (up from 317M, about +10%), of which 304M mobile players; mobile about $1.6B, almost 90% of revenue — [Games Industry Africa, 31 Aug 2026](https://gamesindustryafrica.com/2026/08/31/africas-games-market-set-to-pass-2-billion-as-player-base-surges-beyond-400-million/); [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/mobile-leads-the-charge-in-africas-18-billion-games-market); [Semafor, 5 Feb 2025](https://www.semafor.com/article/02/05/2025/african-gaming-revenues-hit-18b-in-new-record)
- 2025 (later edition, Aug 2026): $1.77B revenue and 369.66M gamers — [Games Industry Africa, 31 Aug 2026](https://gamesindustryafrica.com/2026/08/31/africas-games-market-set-to-pass-2-billion-as-player-base-surges-beyond-400-million/)
- 2026 forecast: $1.98B (+11.9% YoY); 406.25M gamers (+9.9%, +36.6M net); mobile $1.62B, 81.8% of revenue — [Games Industry Africa, 31 Aug 2026](https://gamesindustryafrica.com/2026/08/31/africas-games-market-set-to-pass-2-billion-as-player-base-surges-beyond-400-million/); [PocketGamer.biz (Africa and Middle East, 406M gamers)](https://pocketgamer.biz/africa-and-middle-east-games-market-reaches-85bn-as-african-gamer-base-hits-406m/)
- Historical baseline: Sub-Saharan Africa gamers rose from 77M (2015) to 186M (2021), 95% on mobile (2021 Newzoo/Carry1st report; old data) — [Games Industry Africa, 31 Aug 2026](https://gamesindustryafrica.com/2026/08/31/africas-games-market-set-to-pass-2-billion-as-player-base-surges-beyond-400-million/)

Conflicting continent totals from other publishers
- Maliyo Games/KPMG "2025 Africa Games Industry Report" (Feb 2025): about $1B revenue in 2024, mobile nearly 90%; Africa earns under 1% of $187B global games revenue — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/mobile-growth-drives-africas-games-industry-to-1-billion-in-revenue); [ThisDay, 4 Mar 2025](https://thisdaylive.com/index.php/2025/03/04/kpmg-africas-game-industry-earns-less-than-1-of-187bn-global-revenue)
- SpielFabrique/Xsolla "State of the African Video Game Industry 2026": about $2.29B in 2025, mobile "around 60%" of value, 12.32% CAGR versus about 7.5% global, cloud gaming about 14% CAGR — [MEA Tech Watch](https://meatechwatch.com/?p=46202). Xsolla's own summary of the same report cites about $1.8B in 2024 with mobile close to 90% — [Xsolla blog, 9 Feb 2026](https://xsolla.com/blog/xsolla-state-of-the-african-video-game-industry-report). The two descriptions of one report disagree on mobile share (60% vs 90%).
- A 2025 forecast of $2.39B has also circulated — [Techloy](https://techloy.com/africas-gaming-market-could-hit-2-39-billion-in-2025)

Country level
- Egypt: $368M (2024, Carry1st/Newzoo), largest by revenue, over 20M players; 2026 forecast $458.1M — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/report-egypt-leads-africas-games-market-with-368m-in-revenue); [Games Industry Africa](https://gamesindustryafrica.com/2026/08/31/africas-games-market-set-to-pass-2-billion-as-player-base-surges-beyond-400-million/)
- Nigeria: $300M (2024, Carry1st/Newzoo) or "$250-300M"; about 46.5M gamers, the most on the continent, about 90% on mobile (Maliyo 2025 Nigeria report); 2026 forecast only $226.6M — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/report-egypt-leads-africas-games-market-with-368m-in-revenue); [Games Industry Africa](https://gamesindustryafrica.com/2026/08/31/africas-games-market-set-to-pass-2-billion-as-player-base-surges-beyond-400-million/)
- South Africa: $278M and 26.5M players (2024); 2026 forecast $263.0M, ranked second — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/report-egypt-leads-africas-games-market-with-368m-in-revenue); [Games Industry Africa](https://gamesindustryafrica.com/2026/08/31/africas-games-market-set-to-pass-2-billion-as-player-base-surges-beyond-400-million/)
- Kenya: only an old figure was found, $46M gaming revenue in 2021 (cited in KPMG 2025 report) — [KPMG/Maliyo 2025 Africa Games Industry Report PDF](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmg/ng/pdf/2025/02/2025%20Africa%20Games%20Industry%20Report.pdf)
- Fastest-growing by revenue in the 2024 data: Eritrea and Niger (small bases) — [Semafor, 5 Feb 2025](https://www.semafor.com/article/02/05/2025/african-gaming-revenues-hit-18b-in-new-record)
- Share of adults who play video games (GeoPoll survey, as reported 2026): Egypt 89%, South Africa 81%, Kenya 68%, Nigeria 58% — [Esports Africa News, 8 Jun 2026](https://esportsafricanews.com/?p=10014)
- Esports is small: South Africa $7-10M revenue and up to 3M viewers; Egypt $5-7M — [Esports Africa News, 25 Jan 2026](https://esportsafricanews.com/the-african-games-esport-economy-size-growth-trends-and-untapped-value/)

### Inferences
- Nigeria fell from second ($300M, 2024) to third ($226.6M, 2026 forecast) in USD terms while keeping the largest player base. This is consistent with naira depreciation shrinking dollar revenue rather than with fewer players, but no source states the cause.
- Egypt + South Africa + Nigeria = $947.7M of the $1.98B 2026 forecast, about 48% of the continent.
- Implied average revenue per gamer is about $4.9 a year continent-wide (2026: $1.98B / 406.25M), about $4.9 in Nigeria on 2024 figures ($226.6M / 46.5M, mixing years) versus about $10.5 in South Africa ($278M / 26.5M, 2024). Volume sits in Nigeria; value per player sits in South Africa and Egypt.
- The 2025 restatement ($1.77B after $1.8B for 2024) means "12% growth" claims should be quoted with the edition, not chained across editions.

### Gaps
- No country figures found for Ghana, Morocco or Ethiopia, and nothing newer than 2021 for Kenya. The primary Carry1st/Newzoo PDFs probably hold these.
- No payer counts or paying-share (payer conversion) figures by country were found in 2025-2026 sources.
- The primary reports were not read directly; all figures are via press coverage.

## 2. Who are the players: age, devices, data costs, genres, languages, social platforms?

### Takeaway
The audience is young, over 90% mobile-first and overwhelmingly Android, playing more than an hour a day, and constrained mainly by data cost and disposable income rather than interest. Country-level splits for genre, language and social platform use were not found in open sources.

### Cited Findings
- GeoPoll "Gaming in Africa 2025" (fieldwork Sep-Nov 2025; 6,000+ adult players in Egypt, Kenya, Nigeria, South Africa, Tanzania, Senegal; 55% male, 45% female): 91% name mobile as main platform; about three-quarters play more than one hour a day — [Games Industry Africa, 10 Dec 2025](https://gamesindustryafrica.com/2025/12/10/new-2025-gaming-in-africa-report-reveals-mobile-first-market-and-strong-demand-for-cultural-representation/)
- Same survey: leading barrier is high data costs, second is limited disposable income for in-game spend; nearly one-third use energy-saving modes while gaming — [Games Industry Africa, 10 Dec 2025](https://gamesindustryafrica.com/2025/12/10/new-2025-gaming-in-africa-report-reveals-mobile-first-market-and-strong-demand-for-cultural-representation/)
- Same survey: 59% have made a purchase after seeing in-game advertising; more than half say cultural relevance matters and want more Black characters and African settings; more than half cannot name an African-made game — [Games Industry Africa, 10 Dec 2025](https://gamesindustryafrica.com/2025/12/10/new-2025-gaming-in-africa-report-reveals-mobile-first-market-and-strong-demand-for-cultural-representation/)
- GeoPoll 2024 edition (2,500+ players in Nigeria, Egypt, Kenya, South Africa): 92% play on mobile; 92% report downloading games from Google Play; puzzle is the top genre at 40%, sports/football 36% (not split by country) — [GeoPoll Gaming in Africa report](https://www.geopoll.com/resources/gaming-africa-report/); [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/technology/article/smartphones-youthful-population-to-fuel-africas-gaming-boom/)
- Age: continental median age of about 19.2 cited as the demographic driver (a population figure, not a gamer survey result) — [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/technology/article/smartphones-youthful-population-to-fuel-africas-gaming-boom/)
- Stores: Google Play is the primary marketplace across the continent; Apple's App Store has a smaller but higher-value audience in South Africa, Egypt and Morocco (2026 report) — [MEA Tech Watch](https://meatechwatch.com/?p=46202)
- Handset affordability (GSMA, 2025): a basic internet-enabled device costs 87% of average monthly income for the poorest 20% in Sub-Saharan Africa — [Semafor, 8 Sep 2025](https://www.semafor.com/article/09/08/2025/basic-mobile-phone-costs-hold-back-africas-connectivity-report-says). Other GSMA figures on different baselines: 76% for the poorest 20% in SSA — [Leadership](https://leadership.ng/smartphones-cost-76-of-monthly-income-in-sub-saharan-africa/); 24% of average monthly income, up to 80% for the poorest (Mobile Economy Africa 2026) — [TechCentral](https://techcentral.co.za/gsma-tells-africa-to-copy-south-africa-on-devices/282753/)
- Data cost: mobile data often exceeds 7% of monthly income in SSA versus the UN 2% affordability benchmark — [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/technology/article/rising-smartphone-taxes-data-costs-threaten-300bn-opportunity/)
- Device supply: GSMA and six large African operators proposed minimum specs for $30-40 entry-level 4G smartphones (Oct 2025); a $40 smartphone could connect a further 20M people in SSA — [GSMA press release](https://www.gsma.com/newsroom/press-release/gsma-and-leading-african-operators-propose-minimum-requirements-for-affordable-4g-smartphones/). Sub-$100 smartphone shipments fell 34% (almost 3M units) in Q2 2026 as memory and chipset costs rose (Omdia, via coverage) — [Engineering News, 9 Oct 2026](https://engineeringnews.co.za/article/gsma-warns-of-emerging-global-ai-divide-2026-10-09)
- Top-up demand on the Carry1st WhatsApp shop gives a proxy for titles with paying demand: Call of Duty: Mobile, Free Fire, PUBG Mobile, Mobile Legends: Bang Bang, Roblox, plus Razer Gold, Steam, PSN and Xbox vouchers — [Carry1st blog](https://carry1st.com/blog/level-up-in-seconds-carry1st-launcheswhatsapp-shop-in-nigeria-south-africa)

### Inferences
- Survey genre preference (puzzle, football) and paid top-up demand (shooters, battle royale, MOBA, Roblox) diverge: the casual mass plays free, while spend concentrates in competitive mid-core titles and football (EA SPORTS FC Mobile is a Carry1st partner).
- Low-end Android plus costly data argues for small download sizes, lightweight web shop pages, and channels with zero-rated or bundled data (WhatsApp, telco gamer bundles).
- A 45% female share among adult players in GeoPoll suggests creative and targeting should not default to male-only audiences.
- Rising entry-level handset prices in 2026 could slow new-gamer growth below the 9.9% forecast; growth planning should lean on monetising existing players.

### Gaps
- No per-country data found on languages played or preferred, or on social platform usage by gamers (TikTok, WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook, YouTube). DataReportal country reports would be the next source; not consulted.
- No Android versus iOS share by country, no device RAM or OS-version distribution, no country genre splits.
- No gamer age distribution (only adult samples and a population median).

## 3. How do African players pay, and why do direct-to-consumer web shops matter?

### Takeaway
Cards are a minority instrument (about 18% debit and 3% credit penetration in SSA), mobile money and bank transfer dominate digital payments, and app-store billing reaches only a small slice of players; this is the structural reason local-payment web shops and merchant-of-record gateways exist. No measured, continent-wide payment failure rate was found, only directional evidence.

### Cited Findings
- Card penetration in Sub-Saharan Africa: about 18% of adults have a debit card and 3% a credit card — [WeWire blog](https://blog.wewire.com/stay-wired/cash-card-crypto-what-africas-consumers-are-really-choosing-at-checkout/) (blog citing research; not traced to primary). Credit cards are about 3% of transactions, the lowest of any region — [African Business, May 2026](https://african.business/2026/05/african-banker/is-africa-leapfrogging-credit-and-debit-cards)
- Mobile money: 38B of 41B instant payment transactions in Africa in 2023, worth over $1T; about 50% of e-commerce payments in Kenya — [African Business, May 2026](https://african.business/2026/05/african-banker/is-africa-leapfrogging-credit-and-debit-cards)
- "Limited access to credit cards and app-store payment methods affects nearly 90% of the population" (State of the African Video Game Industry 2026) — [MEA Tech Watch](https://meatechwatch.com/?p=46202)
- Among surveyed players (GeoPoll, Sep-Nov 2025), mobile money and Google Pay now match credit cards as the most common in-game payment methods — [Games Industry Africa, 10 Dec 2025](https://gamesindustryafrica.com/2025/12/10/new-2025-gaming-in-africa-report-reveals-mobile-first-market-and-strong-demand-for-cultural-representation/)
- Payment failure, South Africa: 71% of would-be customers report abandoning a purchase because of a payment failure (Stitch 2025 consumer survey; self-reported behaviour, not a transaction failure rate) — [Stitch](https://stitch.money/blog/report-the-state-of-consumer-payments-in-south-africa)
- Nigeria card friction: banks resumed international naira-card transactions in mid-2025 after blocking them for nearly three years; limits are bank-specific (examples cited: about $1,000 per quarter; Wema $500 per month) and recurring charges can fail after a first success — [Evertry blog (vendor of virtual dollar cards; commercially motivated)](https://evertry.co/blog/why-is-my-atm-card-not-working-for-online-payment/)
- Google and Verve agreed to let Nigerians pay on Google Play with naira Verve cards — [The Paypers](https://thepaypers.com/payments/news/google-verve-to-allow-nigerians-to-pay-with-naira-cards-on-play-store)
- Xsolla (3 Dec 2025) states card declines are common in Nigeria and positions wallet, bank transfer and agent cash as the more stable path; Paga has 20M+ users — [Xsolla blog](https://xsolla.com/blog/xsolla-paga-integration-nigeria-payments)
- Carry1st Pay1st: 120+ local payment methods in one integration across South Africa, Nigeria, Kenya, Egypt, Morocco and Ghana; acts as merchant of record handling compliance, tax, FX and risk with USD settlement; partner logos include Riot (VALORANT), Supercell, Activision (Call of Duty), Garena, MPL, Tinder, Nanobit — [Carry1st Pay1st page](https://www.carry1st.com/pay1st)
- Carry1st Shop: 100+ products payable with 120 methods including bank transfer, mobile money and crypto — [The Fintech Times](https://thefintechtimes.com/carry1st-becomes-one-stop-digital-essentials-shop-for-consumers-across-africa/)
- Carry1st WhatsApp Shop (Nigeria and South Africa; 2026, exact date unconfirmed: one aggregator says 5 Jun 2026, the blog sits among Oct 2026 posts): chat-based catalogue, pay by instant EFT, card or mobile money, voucher delivered in chat; also sells airtime, data, prepaid electricity, Netflix, Spotify and DStv vouchers — [Carry1st blog](https://carry1st.com/blog/level-up-in-seconds-carry1st-launcheswhatsapp-shop-in-nigeria-south-africa)
- Carry1st has shifted its centre of gravity to commerce (Carry1st Shop, Pay1st, Flow1st) — [PocketGamer.biz interview](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/carry1sts-guillaume-noe-esports-is-to-gaming-what-formula-1-is-to-the-car-industry/)
- Carrier billing is promoted by vendors as the main method for the unbanked (vendor marketing, unquantified) — [Digital Virgo](https://digitalvirgo.com/?p=86066)

### Inferences
- With roughly 18% debit and 3% credit card penetration and app stores built around cards, in-app billing structurally excludes most willing payers; a web shop with mobile money, bank transfer and instant EFT expands the addressable payer base rather than merely saving store fees.
- Selling airtime, data and electricity alongside game currency turns the shop into a higher-frequency utility, which lowers reacquisition cost for game top-ups.
- Payment mix must be designed per country: mobile money in Kenya and Ghana, bank transfer and wallets (OPay, PalmPay, Paga) in Nigeria, instant EFT and cards in South Africa, wallets and cash networks in Egypt. Only the Kenya and Nigeria/South Africa parts of this are directly evidenced above.

### Gaps
- No measured card authorisation or decline rates by country, and no Carry1st conversion-uplift figures; the Pay1st page publishes none.
- No sourced 2025-2026 figures specific to M-Pesa, MTN MoMo, OPay or PalmPay user counts, nor for voucher, airtime or carrier-billing share of game payments.
- Pay1st fees and take rate are not public.

## 4. Who competes with or neighbours Carry1st?

### Takeaway
Carry1st sits between three groups: global top-up marketplaces now localising African payments (Codashop, Eneba via dLocal, Razer Gold), games-commerce infrastructure vendors (Xsolla, Coda, Aghanim) wiring in Nigerian and mobile-money rails, and telcos bundling data with cloud gaming. Local studios are small and mostly not direct competitors; Carry1st also resells some "competitor" inventory such as Razer Gold.

### Cited Findings

Top-up and voucher marketplaces
- Codashop (Coda Payments) lists Egypt, Morocco, Kenya, Nigeria and South Africa as African markets — [Codashop international page](https://www.codashop.com/international). Entered Egypt in 2021 with Paymob and a Codashop Global Series esports tournament — [Coda blog](https://coda.co/blog/paymob-and-codashop-facilitate-new-advanced-payments-for-online-gamers-in-egypt). Added BudPay checkout in Nigeria (Dec 2022) — [TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2022/12/12/budpay-partners-with-codashop-to-offer-gamers-in-nigeria-a-seamless-way-to-pay-for-their-games/). Coda is also Riot's payments partner in Southeast Asia — [The Paypers](https://thepaypers.com/payments/news/coda-payments-partners-riot-games-for-payments-services-across-southeast-asia)
- Eneba uses dLocal for mobile money, wallets, bank transfers and local card schemes, is present in 13 emerging markets and is expanding into Africa via mobile payments — [dLocal success story](https://dlocal.com/success-stories/powering-seamless-checkout-for-emerging-market-gamers-eneba-and-dlocal)
- Razer Gold claims 200+ payment methods and 25 local currencies globally (no Africa breakdown) — [Razer Gold partners](https://partners.gold.razer.com/). Carry1st sells Razer Gold in Nigeria, South Africa, Kenya, Ghana, Egypt and Morocco with local payment options — [Carry1st blog](https://carry1st.com/blog/what-nigerian-gamers-should-know-about-razer-gold). Recharge.com also sells Razer Gold in Nigeria and South Africa — [Recharge.com](https://www.recharge.com/en/ng/razer-gold)
- SEAGM: its documented local-payment build-out (20+ methods via Rapyd) covers six Asian countries, none African — [Rapyd case study](https://www.rapyd.net/resource/case-studies/seagm/)

Payments and commerce infrastructure
- Xsolla integrated Paga wallet, bank transfer via Paga and agent cash deposits into Pay Station in Nigeria (3 Dec 2025), with a stated goal to expand alternative payments across Africa — [Xsolla blog](https://xsolla.com/blog/xsolla-paga-integration-nigeria-payments). Xsolla also co-published the "State of the African Video Game Industry 2026" report with SpielFabrique (thought-leadership tactic) — [Xsolla blog, 9 Feb 2026](https://xsolla.com/blog/xsolla-state-of-the-african-video-game-industry-report)
- Aghanim (web shop platform) lists Nigeria status as of July 2026: Google Pay and bank cards confirmed, bank transfer and USSD enabled later, NIBSS still onboarding — [Aghanim docs](https://learn.aghanim.com/articles/2608872218-nigeria-payment-method-availability-and-integration-status)
- dLocal added Nigeria and South Africa to its emerging-markets platform — [dLocal press release](https://www.dlocal.com/zh/press-releases/dlocal-expands-emerging-markets-payments-platform-in-africa-adding-nigeria-and-south-africa/)
- Paystack expanded to Egypt, Rwanda and Cote d'Ivoire (Nov 2023; older) — [Techpoint](https://techpoint.africa/2023/11/02/paystack-expands-to-egypt-rwanda-and-cote-divoire/)

Carry1st's own position and tactics
- Publisher partnerships: EA SPORTS FC Mobile top-ups added to Carry1st Shop (EA joins Activision, Supercell and Riot as partners) — [TechEconomy](https://techeconomy.ng/carry1st-partners-electronic-arts-to-scale-ea-sports-fc-mobile-across-africa/)
- Esports as acquisition: Carry1st Africa Cup for Call of Duty: Mobile, 30-31 Aug 2025, Ikeja, Lagos — [PocketGamer.biz interview](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/carry1sts-guillaume-noe-esports-is-to-gaming-what-formula-1-is-to-the-car-industry/)
- Non-gaming adjacency: Audiomack partnership for streaming payments — [Carry1st press](https://carry1st.com/press/partnership-with-africas-most-popular-music-streaming-audiomack)
- Report co-authorship with Newzoo as a recurring PR and B2B lead tactic — [Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/02/05/2025/african-gaming-revenues-hit-18b-in-new-record)

African studios and publishers
- Africa has about 250 game studios; West Africa rose from 12 (2023) to 57 (2024); three studios reported revenue above $1M in 2023; only 3% have ever received government funding — [PocketGamer.biz on Maliyo/KPMG report](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/mobile-growth-drives-africas-games-industry-to-1-billion-in-revenue); [Games Industry Africa survey](https://gamesindustryafrica.com/2025/01/13/african-games-industry-survey/)
- Named studios in the Maliyo report: Maliyo Games (Nigeria), Leti Arts (Ghana), Kiro'o Games (Cameroon) — [ThisDay, 4 Mar 2025](https://thisdaylive.com/index.php/2025/03/04/kpmg-africas-game-industry-earns-less-than-1-of-187bn-global-revenue)

Telco gaming portals
- MTN South Africa and Telecoming launched Cloudplay (Nov 2024): 340+ games from 50 publishers, no downloads — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/mtn-and-telecoming-partnership-brings-cloud-gaming-to-mobile-in-south-africa); MTN also sells Gamer Bundles with discounted data for partnered titles — [The African Mirror](https://theafricanmirror.africa/news/telcos-race-to-win-over-africas-smartphone-gamers/)
- Vodacom pairs cloud gaming with 5G and GameTime bundles; Safaricom uses daily and "gaming-night" micro-bundles in youth offers; Orange's Sonatel runs Wido Games cloud gaming in West Africa (Aug 2025 coverage) — [The Star Kenya, 14 Aug 2025](https://www.the-star.co.ke/sasa/2025-08-14-smartphone-gamers)

### Inferences
- Carry1st's defensible edge versus Codashop, Eneba and Xsolla is local depth: merchant-of-record status, 120+ methods, first-party publisher deals and on-the-ground esports. Global rivals are closing the payment-method gap in Nigeria specifically (Xsolla-Paga, Aghanim, dLocal), so method count alone will not stay a differentiator.
- Telcos are potential partners more than rivals: they control data bundles and carrier billing, which address the top two barriers (data cost, payment access).
- Local studios are too small to compete for spend; they are potential Pay1st customers and content partners, given players' stated wish for African content.

### Gaps
- No market-share, GMV or traffic data found for any marketplace in Africa. Nothing found on UniPin or Gamivo in Africa, or on local informal resellers (WhatsApp/Instagram/Telegram vendors).
- Nothing specific found for Kucheza, Usiku Games or Qene Games in 2025-2026 sources; nothing on Flutterwave's gaming positioning.
- Carry1st's own revenue, shop users and Pay1st volume are not public; funding was not re-verified (a $27M pre-Series B dates from Jan 2023) — [Techpoint](https://techpoint.africa/2023/01/17/south-african-carry1st-27mpreseriesb/)

## 5. What regulatory, FX, fraud and app-store-policy factors shape growth?

### Takeaway
The policy tailwind is real but so far located outside Africa: alternative billing and link-out rights are court-ordered in the US and rolling out in a few major markets, with no confirmed extension to African storefronts. FX has moved from crisis (naira down about 40% in 2024) to relative stability in 2025, while Nigerian privacy rules tightened in September 2025.

### Cited Findings

App-store policy
- Google Play, US: alternative billing and external links allowed from 29 Oct 2025 under the Epic injunction; applies only in the US; order expires 1 Nov 2027 — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/google-play-opens-up-third-party-payments-in-the-us-marking-a-turning-point-for-developer-revenue); [Neon blog, 29 Oct 2025](https://www.neonpay.com/blog/alternative-payments-are-unlocked-for-android-in-the-us-5-must-dos-for-dtc-in-mobile-games)
- Proposed Epic-Google settlement (Nov 2025): 9% service fee on items that do not materially affect gameplay or 20% on those that do, plus 5% if Google Play Billing is used — [Neon blog, 11 Nov 2025](https://www.neonpay.com/blog/but-what-does-the-google-settlement-actually-mean)
- 2026: Google began rolling out alternative payment options across major markets ahead of final court approval, still charging a service fee — [iClarified](https://www.iclarified.com/101291/google-opens-play-store-to-alternative-payments-under-epic-settlement). No African country appears in the rollout lists found.
- Apple, US: April 2025 contempt order barred any commission on external-link purchases and restrictions on link design; Apple updated guidelines 1 May 2025 — [Paddle](https://www.paddle.com/news/industry/us-judge-orders-apple-allow-external-payment-links). ## 6. What is the ad market like: CPI/CPM on Google, Meta, TikTok; TikTok Ads availability; restrictions?

### Takeaway
TikTok self-serve ad accounts can be created in only five African countries (Egypt, Kenya, Morocco, Nigeria, South Africa), which matches Carry1st's core markets except Ghana. No reliable 2025-2026 CPI or CPM benchmarks for African countries were found in open sources, so cost assumptions must come from first-party tests.

### Cited Findings
- TikTok self-serve ad account creation in Business Center (page updated Nov 2025): African countries listed are Egypt, Kenya, Morocco, Nigeria and South Africa — [TikTok Business Help Center](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/available-countries-and-regions-for-ad-account-creation-in-bc?lang=en)
- Some third-party blogs still claim Nigerian advertisers need an agency account; this conflicts with TikTok's official list and is less authoritative — [House of Marketers](https://www.houseofmarketers.com/?p=6714)
- TikTok Pangle placements are available in Egypt, Kenya, Nigeria and South Africa for managed accounts; for self-serve, registration country determines available locations — [TikTok Pangle locations](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/available-locations-for-pangle-ads)
- Billing friction for local advertisers: Google Ads does not accept naira as a billing currency, and naira cards are commonly declined on Google and TikTok ad billing — [Grey blog (fintech vendor)](https://grey.co/blog/pay-for-google-ads-in-nigeria); [Grey blog on TikTok Ads](https://grey.co/blog/pay-for-tiktok-ads-in-nigeria)
- Creator ecosystem: TikTok creator monetisation is limited in Africa; reports say only Morocco, Egypt and South Africa have access to some reward programmes and no African country is in the general Creator Rewards programme — [OkayAfrica](https://okayafrica.com/tiktok-africa-creator-economy-monetization-exclusion)
- In-game ads convert: 59% of surveyed African players have purchased after seeing in-game advertising (GeoPoll, Sep-Nov 2025) — [Games Industry Africa, 10 Dec 2025](https://gamesindustryafrica.com/2025/12/10/new-2025-gaming-in-africa-report-reveals-mobile-first-market-and-strong-demand-for-cultural-representation/)
- Only dated or non-African benchmark points surfaced: AppsFlyer regional fallback pages show Android hypercasual CPI of $0.06 (Middle East, 2022) and Android match CPI of $0.75 (2023) — [AppsFlyer benchmarks](https://www.appsflyer.com/benchmarks/gaming/hypercasual/hypercasual---overall/android/qatar/2022-08-01/overall). These are Middle East figures and should not be used as African benchmarks.

### Inferences
- Ghana is absent from TikTok's self-serve list, so TikTok activity there would need an account registered in another eligible country or a managed/agency route.
- Because African creators are largely excluded from TikTok's payout programmes, paid creator and affiliate deals (commission on top-ups, voucher codes) are likely to be unusually attractive to them; this is a hypothesis to test.
- Low monetisation per player (about $5 a year average) means paid acquisition only works at very low CPIs or when targeted at existing payers of partner titles; web shop campaigns should be judged on first-purchase cost and repeat rate rather than installs.

### Gaps
- No sourced 2025-2026 CPI, CPM or CPC figures for Nigeria, South Africa, Egypt, Kenya, Ghana or Morocco on Google, Meta or TikTok. AppsFlyer, Adjust and Liftoff public pages returned nothing Africa-specific; paid benchmark tools were not accessed.
- No sourced detail on Meta or Google ad policy restrictions specific to these countries (for example real-money gaming or financial-product rules), nor on Meta ad billing in local currency.
- No data on share of ad spend or user reach by platform per country.
